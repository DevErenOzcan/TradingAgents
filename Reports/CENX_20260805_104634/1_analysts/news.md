### **2. Key Catalysts and Company-Specific Developments**

#### **A. Mt. Holly Smelter Expansion & Government Support**
In late July/early August 2026, Century Aluminum celebrated the expansion of its **Mt. Holly smelter in South Carolina**. 
* **Capacity Increase:** The upgrade lifts total U.S. primary aluminum production capacity by approximately **10%**, representing a substantial volume boost for the company. The plant's workforce has grown to over 600 employees.
* **Trade Policy Integration:** This expansion is deeply intertwined with **Section 232 national security tariffs** on aluminum imports. High-profile government officials joined company leadership to mark the occasion, signaling strong bipartisan and executive backing for protective trade measures that insulate domestic producers from cheaper, subsidized foreign competition (primarily from China).

#### **B. AI Data Center Infrastructure Option & The Hawesville Legacy**
A highly notable development in July 2026 was the announcement that AI heavyweight **Anthropic** signed a **$19 billion, 20-year lease agreement with TeraWulf** to build a massive AI data center campus at the former Century Aluminum smelter site in Hawesville, Kentucky.
* **Context:** Century sold this idled smelter to TeraWulf earlier this year for $200 million. 
* **Implication:** While `CENX` has already monetized this specific asset, the eye-popping $19 billion lease highlights a critical macro trend: **decommissioned metal smelters possess exceptionally valuable power-grid infrastructure** (high-voltage substations, massive transmission access). This establishes a premium valuation floor for `CENX`'s legacy assets and suggests that its existing and future footprints (like the planned greenfield smelter in Oklahoma) carry substantial optionality value beyond pure metal production.

#### **C. Growth Runway & Low-Carbon Supply Chain**
* **Oklahoma Greenfield Smelter:** At the Wells Fargo Industrials and Materials Conference, management framed its long-term growth around a planned **new greenfield smelter in Oklahoma**, capitalizing on tight regional markets and tariffs.
* **Brimstone MoU:** `CENX` signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with Brimstone to source low-carbon alumina from Brimstone’s planned domestic facility. This positions `CENX` to meet the rapidly rising corporate demand for "green aluminum" in automotive and consumer electronics.

### **3. Macroeconomic and Geopolitical Backdrop**

#### **A. Federal Reserve & Market Volatility**
Following the late-July 2026 Federal Reserve meeting, the markets experienced "Fed Day Turmoil" but quickly bounced back. While high interest rates have pressured capital-intensive sectors over the medium term, the underlying resilience of the U.S. economy keeps industrial demand steady.

#### **B. Energy Costs Easing**
Aluminum smelting is highly electricity-intensive, making energy the primary variable operating cost. 
* **Sliding Oil Prices:** Crude oil prices have eased recently as investors monitor U.S.-Iran negotiations and Mideast peace initiatives. 
* **Impact on CENX:** A reduction in global fossil fuel and energy benchmarks provides critical cost relief to `CENX`’s electricity-dependent smelters, helping to protect margins ahead of Q2 earnings.

#### **C. Strategic Metals and Global Trade Barriers**
The geopolitical "rare earth war" continues, with the U.S. Army partnering with domestic rare earth firms to counter Chinese export restrictions. Similar national security dynamics apply to aluminum. Recent executive orders targeting defense supply chains directly encourage the domestic sourcing of metals, positioning `CENX` as a primary national security beneficiary.
